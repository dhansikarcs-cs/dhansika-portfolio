## SENTINEL

### AI · Physiological Signals · Mental Healthcare · Systems

[View Sentinel on GitHub](https://github.com/dhansikarcs-cs/sentinel-mental-health)

Sentinel is an AI-powered mental-health technology platform I am developing as a broader clinical-support system rather than simply an AI application.

The system combines software-based analysis and workflows with connections to physiological signals. It brings together journal and emotional analysis, clinician-oriented triage and risk signals, structured workflows, explainable analysis, privacy-conscious data handling, security considerations, and a developing hardware layer for physiological sensing.

The MVP and core workflow are complete. I have tested the system through simulated scenarios and conducted security testing, while receiving meaningful external feedback from professionals across psychology and medicine.

The goal is not to replace mental-health professionals, but to explore how technology can support better-informed human decision-making.

# RESEARCH

## MACHINE LEARNING FOR NETWORK SECURITY

### Machine Learning · Cybersecurity · Robustness

My current research examines what happens when machine-learning intrusion detection systems encounter data that differs from the distribution they were trained on.

Using CICIDS2017, I evaluate **Logistic Regression, Random Forest, XGBoost, and a Multi-Layer Perceptron** across conventional evaluation and multiple distribution-shift scenarios, including session and attack-family shifts, leave-one-out evaluation, and synthetic feature perturbation.

The research demonstrates how models that achieve near-perfect performance under conventional random splits can experience severe degradation under distribution shift, highlighting the difference between benchmark performance and realistic deployment robustness.

The paper is currently being completed.

**Independent Research · Experimental Design · Machine Learning · Network Security · Data Analysis**

# HOW I WORK

## QUESTION → RESEARCH → THINK → DESIGN → BUILD → TEST → REFLECT → COMMUNICATE

I tend to start by understanding the problem before deciding how to implement a solution.

I break complex problems into systems, identify their assumptions and constraints, research what is already known, and design an approach before building.

AI is part of my current development workflow, particularly for implementation assistance, experimentation, and testing. I review generated work, understand the architecture and security implications, and use each project to strengthen my underlying programming and engineering ability.

I don't consider a system finished simply because it runs.

I want to understand:

**Why does it work?**
**What assumptions does it depend on?**
**Where could it fail?**
**What happens when those assumptions change?**

That approach is reflected in both my research and my projects.
